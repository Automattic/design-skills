# Channels & structures — duration targets, platform grammars, retention

Load when the purpose/channel is known or when choosing a structure.

## Duration targets by purpose

| Purpose | Duration | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage hero | 60–90s | Problem → solution → proof window where engagement, retention, and conversion overlap |
| Organic social | 15–60s | Short-form grammar; hook decides everything |
| Product page | 90s–2min | Retention holds above 70% under a minute and drops sharply past two — front-load value |
| Paid ads | 15–30s | Hook + payoff + CTA, nothing else |
| Sales enablement | 2–3min | Still one message; sections over chapters |
| Internal training | 3–5min | Well past the drop-off; chunk it into sections |

## The hook window

- The first 1–3 seconds carry the watch-or-scroll decision: industry reports consistently put most scroll decisions inside that window.
- The practical rule: if more than 30% of viewers leave before the 3-second mark, rewrite the hook rather than reshoot.
- For explainers, the first 5 seconds decide survival: state the problem or payoff immediately. **Never open with a logo animation, company introduction, or slow stock-footage pan.**
- 3-second hook micro-structure: second 1 = pattern interrupt; second 2 = clear benefit or tease; second 3 = proof or preview of value.
- Mechanisms that work: pattern interruption, curiosity gap, social proof. High-performing hook categories: frustration-targeting questions, contrarian declaratives, mid-story starts, quantified tutorials ("[outcome] in under [time]"), numbers/social proof, warnings, relatable emotional hooks.

## Tested structures

**60-second explainer timeline:** 0–5s hook → 5–25s problem in the viewer's context → 25–45s solution and how it works → 45–55s social proof / key result → 55–60s CTA.

**10-10-10 (ultra-short):** 10 words for the hook → 10 seconds delivering value → 10 seconds for the CTA / next step.

**S.T.A.R.T.:** Stop the scroll → Tease the payoff → Authority in a line → Reward fast (a mini-win within 5–15s) → Tell them what's next.

**Story Snap:** a micro-story in 20 seconds — one problem, one turning point, one lesson.

**Seven-pattern library:** Problem–Myth–Fix · Template–Demo–CTA · Before–After–Bridge · Listicle Lightning · Story Snap · Comparison Cut · Challenge Hook.

**Binding disciplines (all structures):**
- Three key points max — one problem, one core solution, one proof point.
- Something changes every 2–3 seconds (cut, punch-in, on-screen text, B-roll, SFX) — or at minimum a pause, tone change, or voice variation every ~5 seconds. In the script, mark these as visual notes or tonal shifts so the editor can see the cadence.

## The loop economy (Shorts)

- YouTube counts every replay of a Short as a view (since March 31, 2025); repeat viewing is a strong quality signal.
- Loop mechanics: callback hook (the ending echoes the opening question), visual match cut hiding the seam, audio continuing across the loop point with no gap or closing note.
- Keep loopable Shorts at 20–25 seconds (highest completion rates). **Cut the spoken CTA** — a 3-second "follow for more" is 15% of a 20-second Short and breaks the loop; the CTA belongs in the caption.
- Faceless voiceover videos have a structural loop advantage: no face or body cues signal a restart.

## Platform grammars

| Platform | Bias | Optimize for | CTA style |
|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok | Discovery-first, curiosity-driven | Replays, shares (curiosity signals) | Participation ("Stitch this with your version") |
| Reels | Familiarity, clarity, utility | Saves | Utility ("Save this for later") |
| YouTube (long) | Watch-time, retention | Completion, looped Shorts replays | Verbal CTA + description links |
| Explainer/product | Conversion | 60–90s completion | Direct next step |

The same brand opens with a curiosity hook on TikTok and a utility hook on Reels. Write the platform variant into the script's channel field, not a separate script.

## Micro-CTAs and measurement

- Stack micro-CTAs: a soft ask around second 5 ("if this helps, tap save"), the hard ask in the final line.
- Replays = curiosity achieved (TikTok-weighted) · saves = utility (Reels-weighted) · shares = value transfer · comments = a clear prompt worked.
- Burn-in subtitles still matter: around 75% of people watch mobile video on mute, and captions aid comprehension even sound-on.
- Benchmark retention graphs at the 3-second and 15-second marks 48 hours after upload, then rewrite the hook if the graph says so.
